Banyana Banyana's WAFCON Morocco 2026 Campaign: Targets, Expectations and Mood

Four years after lifting their maiden continental crown in Morocco, Banyana Banyana are back — chasing redemption after a disappointing 2024 and a place at the 2027 World Cup.

📅 26 July 2026 ✍️ Kgomotso Nigel Peu, Independent Sports Journalist 🏷️ Banyana Banyana, WAFCON, Women's Football ⏱️ 6 min read

Banyana Banyana have arrived in Morocco with a full complement of players and a clear sense of purpose. The 2026 TotalEnergies CAF Women's Africa Cup of Nations runs from 26 July to 16 August, and South Africa open their campaign against Tanzania in Casablanca on Monday, 27 July. Four years on from winning their first-ever continental title on Moroccan soil, Dr Desiree Ellis's side return to the same country determined to erase the memory of a fourth-place finish at the 2024 edition and reassert themselves among Africa's elite.

The Squad: Experience Meets Fresh Faces

Head coach Desiree Ellis has named a 26-player squad blending the core of her title-winning group with a handful of new faces. Striker Thembi Kgatlana returns as the attacking spearhead after scoring the decisive goal in South Africa's qualifying win over the Democratic Republic of Congo, while goalkeepers Kaylin Swart, Andile Dlamini and Kebotseng Moletsane provide strength in depth between the posts. Midfielder Linda Motlhalo, who plays her club football in Scotland, arrives one appearance short of a century of caps for her country — a landmark that could arrive during the group stage. The squad also includes the return of Robyn Moodaly-Salgado, back in the fold after last representing Banyana Banyana in 2023, and the inclusion of 18-year-old forward Zoe October, the youngest player in the travelling party and a first-time major tournament call-up. South Africa's defensive unit leans heavily on players from Mamelodi Sundowns Ladies, reflecting that club's continued dominance of the domestic women's game.

The Draw: Group B

South Africa have been drawn into Group B alongside Tanzania, Côte d'Ivoire and Burkina Faso. The opening match against Tanzania in Casablanca is being treated internally as a pivotal fixture — a win would put Banyana Banyana in control of their own destiny early in the tournament, while any slip-up would immediately increase the pressure heading into games against Ivorian and Burkinabé opposition. Sixteen teams are competing at this year's tournament, with the top four finishers automatically qualifying for the 2027 FIFA Women's World Cup in Brazil.

The Road to Morocco

South Africa's route to this tournament came with a scare. As one of the six highest-ranked teams in the FIFA Women's World Ranking on the continent, Banyana Banyana received a bye straight to the second round of qualifying, where they were held to a 1-1 draw away to the Democratic Republic of Congo. They needed a response at home, and got one: a 1-0 win in Soweto, settled by a trademark finish from Kgatlana, sent South Africa through to Morocco as one of the tournament's 16 qualifiers.

Targets and Expectations

Publicly, Ellis has been careful to frame the mission in two parts. The immediate, non-negotiable target is a top-four finish to secure World Cup qualification for Brazil 2027 — a goal she has repeated consistently in the build-up. Reclaiming the continental title itself, four years after South Africa's historic triumph over hosts Morocco in the 2022 final, remains the standing ambition for a squad that has now finished as African runners-up on four separate occasions (2000, 2008, 2012 and 2018) either side of that breakthrough win. With fixtures now confirmed, Ellis said preparations could go "full blast," stressing that the mission remained the same: qualifying for the World Cup in Brazil.

The Mood in Camp

Despite the weight of a disappointing 2024 campaign hanging over the squad, the mood in the South African camp is described as confident rather than anxious. Ellis has acknowledged the tournament will present a difficult challenge but says her coaching staff trust the squad has the quality and depth to compete with Africa's strongest sides, while being open about the fact that players understand the expectations placed on them by supporters back home. Returning midfielder Robyn Moodaly-Salgado captured some of that renewed energy in camp, saying she was thrilled to be back in national colours with "new expectations, new goals, new year." For a squad that has become one of the continent's most consistent performers over the past two decades, there is a clear sense that this WAFCON represents both a chance at redemption and an opportunity to build toward next year's World Cup.

Why This Tournament Matters Beyond the Trophy

There is also a broader significance to this campaign for South African football as a whole. A strong showing in Morocco would arrive in the same year that Bafana Bafana made history at the men's World Cup, giving South African football a genuine claim to describe 2026 as one of its most successful years across both the men's and women's game. For a federation working hard to grow the profile and commercial backing of women's football domestically, Banyana Banyana's results in Morocco carry weight well beyond the group stage table.
