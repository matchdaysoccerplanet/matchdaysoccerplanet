Who Should Replace Hugo Broos? Mosimane, McCarthy and Mokwena Lead the Race

SAFA now faces the most important appointment in South African football's modern era. We break down the leading candidates.

📅 10 July 2026 ✍️ Matchday Soccer Planet Editorial 🏷️ Bafana Bafana, SAFA, Coaching ⏱️ 5 min read

Hugo Broos has confirmed his departure as Bafana Bafana head coach, telling Belgian magazine Humo that he has already spoken with SAFA president Danny Jordaan about a different role — as an advisor rather than head coach. Broos indicated he plans to return to South Africa at the end of July to say his final goodbyes. Although SAFA officially dismissed reports of his exit on 10 July, the 74-year-old has made clear to Belgian media that his coaching career is over.

A Legacy Built on Substance

Broos leaves as the longest-serving Bafana coach since South Africa's re-admission to international football in 1992. His legacy includes an AFCON bronze medal in 2024 and, above all, guiding South Africa to the knockout stage of a World Cup for the first time in the nation's history — a genuine watershed moment for South African football.

The Three Names Dominating the Conversation

1. Favourite

Pitso Mosimane

His trophy cabinet is unmatched among South African coaches: multiple PSL titles with Mamelodi Sundowns, the CAF Champions League with both Sundowns and Al Ahly, and a third-place finish at the Club World Cup. The open question is whether he would leave lucrative club football for the pressures — and comparatively modest pay — of the national job.

2. Fan Favourite

Benni McCarthy

Currently coaching Kenya, the Orlando Pirates and Bafana legend would be a hugely popular appointment with supporters. He guided AmaZulu to a historic second-place Betway Premiership finish and CAF Champions League qualification before his current post. Reports from The Sowetan suggest he has expressed genuine interest in the role.

3. Tactical Choice

Rulani Mokwena

The former Mamelodi Sundowns co-coach, who won four consecutive PSL titles, has also reportedly expressed interest. His tactical intelligence is widely respected, and his experience across both the Betway Premiership and North African football (Wydad AC, MC Alger) gives him a broad, credible profile for the national setup.

Helman Mkhalele, Broos's longtime assistant, is also in the frame as a continuity option who understands the current squad intimately.

What a New Coach Inherits

Whoever SAFA appoints will inherit a squad in a stronger position than at any point in recent memory. Relebohile Mofokeng has already completed a reported R70 million move to Belgian side Royale Union Saint-Gilloise, while Teboho Mokoena, Thapelo Maseko and Khuliso Mudau all enhanced their reputations during the World Cup campaign. Domestically, Orlando Pirates completed a historic treble and Mamelodi Sundowns retained their CAF Champions League crown, meaning the new head coach will be picking from a player pool drawn from two of the continent's in-form clubs as well as an expanding contingent based in Europe.

A Federation Under Scrutiny

The appointment also comes with a level of public scrutiny that SAFA has not faced in years. Broos's transformation of the team's fortunes has raised expectations rather than settled them, and fans who watched Bafana Bafana reach a World Cup knockout stage for the first time will expect any successor to build on that platform rather than merely maintain it. That makes the timing and process of this appointment arguably more consequential than any national team coaching search since South Africa's return to international football in 1992.

The Clock Is Ticking

SAFA must make its decision before the AFCON 2027 qualifiers begin in September 2026 — leaving only a short window to appoint a successor and allow them time to assess the current squad, many of whom are now attracting serious attention from clubs across Europe following the historic World Cup run.

Continuity vs a Clean Break

Part of what makes this decision so difficult for SAFA is the genuine tension between two competing philosophies. Appointing someone from within Broos's existing coaching set-up, such as long-serving assistant Helman Mkhalele, would offer continuity and minimise disruption to a squad that has just enjoyed its best-ever tournament together. Bringing in an established, decorated figure from outside the current set-up, by contrast, risks a period of tactical adjustment for players who have grown used to a settled system, but could also inject fresh ideas at a moment when other African nations will be studying South Africa's World Cup run closely and looking to close the gap.

The Financial Reality

Whoever SAFA appoints will also need to be someone the financially stretched federation can actually afford. South African football's showpiece coaching job does not command the sort of budget available to wealthier football nations, and any negotiations with high-profile candidates currently earning club salaries abroad or at CAF Champions League level will need to account for that reality. It is one reason names already based in the domestic game, or with a demonstrated willingness to prioritise the national team job over maximum earnings, tend to feature so prominently in the conversation.
