Neo Mokoena Leaves Orlando Pirates: Why the DDC Prospect Was Released

A three-year contract, a direct comparison to Mofokeng, and still no first-team minutes — the inside story of why the Buccaneers and their young winger have gone their separate ways.

📅 26 July 2026 ✍️ Kgomotso Nigel Peu, Independent Sports Journalist 🏷️ Orlando Pirates, Transfers, Betway Premiership ⏱️ 5 min read

Orlando Pirates and winger Neo Mokoena have mutually agreed to part ways, ending a promising but ultimately frustrating first-team spell for the 24-year-old that never really got going. His representatives, P Management, have confirmed he is now a free agent and have begun the search for his next club — though no destination has been confirmed at the time of writing.

From DDC Breakout to First-Team Promise

Mokoena's rise at Orlando Pirates followed a familiar and encouraging path. He came through the club's DStv Diski Challenge (DDC) development side, where his pace and directness on the wing caught the eye of the club's coaching staff. He wore the number 15 shirt in the DDC — the same number once worn by a young Relebohile Mofokeng before his own breakthrough — and the comparisons between the two attacking prospects followed him for much of 2025.

In July 2025, ahead of the 2025/26 Betway Premiership season, Pirates rewarded that development with a three-year first-team contract, promoting Mokoena permanently after impressive performances in the reserve set-up. The timing was significant: the club had just sold breakout winger Mohau Nkota to Saudi Pro League side Al-Ettifaq, and Mokoena was widely tipped as his long-term successor on the flank.

Why He Left: Squeezed Out By a Star-Studded Squad

The promotion, however, never translated into senior game time. Pirates spent the 2025/26 season assembling one of the deepest and most expensively stocked squads in the Betway Premiership under head coach Abdeslam Ouaddou, a squad that went on to complete a historic domestic treble. With established attacking options such as Oswin Appollis, Tshepang Moremi and Deon Hotto ahead of him in the pecking order, Mokoena could not force his way into Ouaddou's matchday plans.

The situation worsened as the season went on. By late 2025, reports emerged that Mokoena was no longer even featuring for Pirates' first team in cup competitions, but had instead dropped down to play for the club's feeder side in the ABC Motsepe League — a development tier several rungs below the Betway Premiership. For a player who had signed a three-year top-flight contract only months earlier, it was a significant step backwards.

With his opportunities drying up and his development stalling, the two parties have now mutually agreed to part ways rather than have Mokoena spend the remainder of his contract on the periphery of the squad.

The Mofokeng Contrast

The comparison to Mofokeng, once a lighthearted talking point among Pirates fans, now reads as a study in contrasts. Both wingers wore the number 15 in the DDC. Both were touted as the club's next academy graduate to make the step up. But while Mofokeng went on to become a Bafana Bafana star at the 2026 FIFA World Cup and completed a reported R70 million move to Belgian side Royale Union Saint-Gilloise this month, Mokoena could not find a single senior appearance in the same squad. It is a reminder of how narrow the pathway to regular first-team football can be, even for academy graduates handed long-term contracts at one of South Africa's biggest clubs.

What's Next

P Management say they have already begun searching for a new home for their client, and given his pedigree at DDC level, interest from elsewhere in the Betway Premiership — or a move to a club that can offer regular senior football — would not be a surprise. For now, though, Mokoena's next destination remains unconfirmed, and Matchday Soccer Planet will update this story as soon as a move is official.

A Broader Pattern in South African Football

Mokoena's situation is not an isolated one. Every season, promising academy graduates across the Betway Premiership sign senior contracts on the back of standout underage form, only to discover that the gap between reserve-team football and a first-team matchday squad at a trophy-chasing club can be enormous. Squad depth at clubs the size of Orlando Pirates means even technically gifted young players can go an entire season without a single minute of senior football if the timing, tactical fit or competition for places does not break in their favour. Mokoena's release is a reminder that a long-term contract, on its own, guarantees opportunity far less often than fans might assume.

The Road Back

For a player of Mokoena's underage pedigree, a move to another Betway Premiership club — potentially one lower down the table with a clearer pathway to regular football — would represent the most logical next step. Rebuilding form and confidence through consistent senior minutes elsewhere would give him the best chance of one day earning a recall to a bigger club, following a well-trodden path used by other South African players who found their initial big move came too early in their development.
