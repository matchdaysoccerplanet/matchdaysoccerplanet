Bafana Bafana at World Cup 2026: The Full Campaign Story

Mexico. Czechia. South Korea. Canada. The match-by-match story of how South Africa made history — reaching a World Cup knockout stage for the very first time.

What a campaign. Bafana Bafana's return to the World Cup — their first appearance since hosting the tournament in 2010 — ended in the Round of 32, but not before the team made history by reaching the knockout stage of a World Cup for the very first time. This is the complete story of four matches that will be remembered for a generation.

Matchday 1: South Africa 0–2 Mexico

Group A

Mexico 2–0 South Africa

11 June 2026 · Estadio Azteca, Mexico City

South Africa's return to the World Cup stage began with a difficult night against co-hosts Mexico in front of a raucous Estadio Azteca crowd. Julián Quiñones gave Mexico an early lead in the 9th minute, and Raúl Jiménez doubled the advantage in the 67th minute. The match was also notable for three red cards — two shown to South African players and one to Mexico's César Montes in stoppage time.

Despite the numerical disadvantage for long periods, South Africa never seriously threatened Mexico's goal. Speaking afterward, Hugo Broos struck a defiant tone: "It was a difficult day. We have two more games and must show our character." As the campaign unfolded, that character would indeed shine through.

Matchday 2: Czechia 1–1 South Africa

Group A

Czechia 1–1 South Africa

18 June 2026 · Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Atlanta

South Africa showed tremendous character at Mercedes-Benz Stadium to earn a point that kept their World Cup hopes alive. Michal Sadilek gave Czechia the earliest lead of the tournament so far, scoring inside six minutes. South Africa dominated the second half, out-creating Czechia by expected goals (1.37 to 1.02), and were rewarded when the referee awarded a penalty after Thapelo Maseko's shot struck the arm of Sulc in the penalty area.

Teboho Mokoena stepped up and coolly converted from the spot in the 83rd minute to earn a crucial point — a moment made more remarkable by the fact that Mokoena's Bafana teammate Jayden Adams had been substituted at half-time after learning his grandmother had passed away the day before. Both sides finished level on 1 point each from two games, setting up a winner-takes-all final matchday against South Korea.

Matchday 3: South Africa 1–0 South Korea — History Made

Group A · Decisive

South Africa 1–0 South Korea

24 June 2026 · Monterrey Stadium, Monterrey

The scenes at Monterrey Stadium were extraordinary. When Thapelo Maseko collected the ball on the left side of the penalty area in the 63rd minute and curled a clinical left-footed finish into the far corner, South Africa made World Cup history — reaching the knockout rounds of the tournament for the very first time.

South Korea, who had left captain Son Heung-min out of the starting XI in a shock call by their coach, dominated possession (68.4%) but could barely create a clear chance against a resolute Bafana defence, managing just 1.0 expected goals compared to South Africa's 1.1.

"For the first time in our history. This is a great day for South African football." — Hugo Broos, after South Africa's 1–0 win over South Korea

South Africa finished second in Group A with 4 points, setting up a Round of 32 clash with co-hosts Canada in Los Angeles.

Round of 32: South Africa 0–1 Canada

Round of 32

South Africa 0–1 Canada

28 June 2026 · SoFi Stadium, Los Angeles

After the elation of beating South Korea, Bafana Bafana's extraordinary World Cup came to a heartbreaking end in Los Angeles. Canada's Stephen Eustaquio received the ball on the edge of the South Africa penalty area in the 92nd minute, chested it down, and rifled a perfect finish into the bottom corner past a diving Ronwen Williams.

For 90 minutes South Africa had defended superbly, frustrating a Canada side that had registered 22 shots on target across the group stage. Williams made several excellent saves and the game seemed destined for extra time — until Eustaquio's moment of brilliance.

Canada advanced to the Round of 16. South Africa exited having made history — reaching the knockouts for the first time ever and winning a World Cup match for the first time — giving South African football real momentum to build on.

"We look back on this World Cup with happiness." — Hugo Broos, after confirming this would be his final match in charge

The Legacy of This Campaign

Teboho Mokoena's cool penalty against Czechia and Thapelo Maseko's stunning 63rd-minute strike against South Korea were the moments that defined the campaign. Khuliso Mudau was outstanding throughout all four games, and the squad's collective character under intense pressure has already reshaped how South African football is perceived internationally.

Hugo Broos confirmed his departure after the Canada match, closing out a five-year tenure that delivered an AFCON bronze medal and, now, a first-ever World Cup knockout appearance. SAFA's search for his successor — and the wave of European transfer interest in the squad that followed — are already reshaping the next chapter for Bafana Bafana.
