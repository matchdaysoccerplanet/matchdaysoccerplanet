Mamelodi Sundowns New Signings 2026/27: Every Arrival So Far

Two confirmed signings, both already on tour in Europe, and a DDC striker who has forced his way into Miguel Cardoso's plans.

📅 26 July 2026 ✍️ Kgomotso Nigel Peu, Independent Sports Journalist 🏷️ Mamelodi Sundowns, Transfers, Betway Premiership ⏱️ 6 min read

Mamelodi Sundowns head into the 2026/27 season defending their CAF Champions League crown while also looking to reclaim the Betway Premiership title they surrendered to Orlando Pirates by a single point last season. So far, head coach Miguel Cardoso has completed two confirmed signings, with the club's own supporters told that "the chequebook is still open" as the transfer window continues.

Who's the Most Valuable Addition?

Antonio van Wyk arrives as the more proven, ready-made option of the two. Over two seasons in Austria he made 53 appearances for SV Ried, scoring four goals and laying on seven assists, and played a direct role in helping the club earn promotion to the Austrian Bundesliga. That European experience — and the fact he has already had time to work with Cardoso's coaching staff before a ball has been kicked in anger — makes him the signing most likely to start the season in the first XI.

Who's Set to Make an Immediate Impact?

Van Wyk has already answered that question in pre-season, scoring in a 1-1 draw with German second-tier side Hertha Berlin during Sundowns' Austrian tour. Siyanda Ndlovu, described by Sundowns-watchers as a "quality" and versatile attacking addition, has also travelled with the squad and is expected to provide competition across multiple forward positions once the season begins. Both players benefit from arriving early enough to fully absorb Cardoso's playing philosophy before facing competitive fixtures — a rare luxury compared to signings who join a squad mid-season.

Don't Forget the Academy: A DDC Striker Already Among the Goals

Sundowns' most eye-catching addition to first-team affairs this year may not be a transfer market signing at all. Nineteen-year-old striker Bennet Mokoena capped an explosive season with Sundowns' DStv Diski Challenge side — finishing as the competition's top scorer with 22 goals in 26 matches — before Cardoso handed him his professional debut against Golden Arrows in September 2025. Mokoena made two Betway Premiership appearances that season and signed off in style, playing the full 90 minutes and scoring in a 3-1 Red Bull Diski Last Namba win over Bundesliga side RB Leipzig. Now a permanent member of the senior set-up, the Vaal-born forward has set himself the target of becoming a regular contributor under Cardoso in 2026/27 — continuing a Sundowns tradition of trusting academy graduates that has already produced Bafana Bafana internationals such as Teboho Mokoena and Aubrey Modiba.

A Squad Still Processing Loss

Sundowns' recruitment this window has taken place against a difficult backdrop. The club is continuing its preparations only weeks after the sudden passing of midfielder Jayden Adams in July, and manager Cardoso has had to balance integrating new arrivals with a squad still processing that loss. Results in preseason have reflected the disruption — Sundowns were beaten 2-0 by Saudi giants Al Hilal in a heavily rotated line-up during the same Austrian tour — but the club's ability to continue functioning, blood new faces, and give a teenage academy graduate his chance says something about the depth and resilience Cardoso has built into the squad.

What's Next

With the transfer window still open, Sundowns are expected to remain active in the market as they prepare to defend their continental crown alongside a renewed domestic title push. Any further business is likely to focus on areas of the squad rotated out for the Al Hilal friendly, including options at full-back and in central midfield, as Cardoso finalises his group ahead of a season played on two demanding fronts.

A Deliberately Different Approach

Compared to Kaizer Chiefs' four signings and Orlando Pirates' eight, Sundowns' two-signing window so far reflects a club operating from a position of relative stability rather than urgent need. As reigning CAF Champions League holders with a squad that finished PSL runners-up by a single point, Cardoso's priority appears to be targeted reinforcement rather than wholesale change — adding proven quality in specific areas while trusting the core of a title-challenging, continentally successful squad to carry over its form into the new campaign. Whether that more conservative approach proves wiser than the busier rebuilds under way at Naturena and Mayfair will become clearer once the season is under way.

The Weight of Two Fronts

Whatever further business Sundowns complete before the window closes, Cardoso's central challenge for 2026/27 remains squad management across two demanding competitions simultaneously. Balancing a CAF Champions League title defence — which will include long-distance continental travel and fixture congestion — against a renewed push to reclaim the Betway Premiership from Orlando Pirates will test squad depth in ways a purely domestic campaign never could. That reality makes the futures of fringe players, and any late-window additions, just as significant to Sundowns' season as the two confirmed arrivals already in the building.
