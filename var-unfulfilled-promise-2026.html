The Unfulfilled Promise of VAR in South African Football

Government funding, ring-fenced accounts, and years of promises — yet the 2026/27 Betway Premiership season will kick off with no Video Assistant Referee system in place.

📅 26 July 2026 ✍️ Kgomotso Nigel Peu, Independent Sports Journalist 🏷️ SAFA, Betway Premiership, Officiating ⏱️ 6 min read

Let's correct the record: there is no Video Assistant Referee system in South African domestic football. Despite years of promises, a confirmed government funding transfer, and a ring-fenced account sitting at the South African Football Association, the Betway Premiership will kick off its 2026/27 season on Saturday, 1 August 2026 exactly as it has every season before it — without VAR. SAFA confirmed this month that the technology will not be introduced this season either, extending a saga that has now stretched across multiple ministers, multiple promised launch dates, and a great deal of frustrated fans.

The Latest Word: Still Not Ready

Speaking in Pretoria this month, SAFA's head of referees, Abdul Ebrahim, gave the most direct answer yet. Asked whether VAR would arrive for the new season, he said plainly: "My simple answer is no." Ebrahim explained that the extensive referee training required to operate the system has not even begun, and that SAFA's project team is still awaiting sign-off from the federation's National Executive Committee before it can even schedule a media briefing to outline next steps. It is, by any measure, a long way from a functioning system — and a striking admission given how far along the project had reportedly progressed just months earlier.

A Timeline of Promises

May 2025
Sports Minister Gayton McKenzie publicly promises that VAR technology will be implemented in South African football "from next season" — a pledge that would ultimately go unmet for the 2025/26 campaign.
May 2026
SAFA confirms it has received roughly R20 million in government funding for the VAR rollout. Finance committee chairperson Mxolisi Sibam says the federation has made "great strides" and praises the government's generosity, fuelling fresh optimism ahead of the new season.
July 2026
Following a preseason FIFA-SAFA referees' workshop, head of referees Abdul Ebrahim confirms there will be no VAR rollout for 2026/27, citing training that has not yet started and a proposal still awaiting National Executive Committee approval.

The Funding Paradox

What makes this delay particularly hard for fans to accept is that the money is, by SAFA's own account, already sitting in an account earmarked specifically for this purpose. The government transfer was confirmed and welcomed publicly by SAFA's own finance leadership. Yet more than a year after Minister McKenzie's original promise, referees have still not begun the specialised training required to operate the system, and the federation itself says it does not yet have internal approval to proceed. The bottleneck, in other words, is not primarily financial — it is administrative and logistical, which for many supporters makes the repeated delays feel even more avoidable.

An Awkward Contrast on the Continent

The delay is made more pointed by developments at continental level. CAF president Patrice Motsepe confirmed earlier this year that Video Assistant Referee technology would be extended to the group stages of the CAF Champions League and CAF Confederation Cup, rather than being reserved for knockout rounds only. That means Mamelodi Sundowns' triumphant 2025/26 CAF Champions League campaign was very likely officiated with VAR support at continental level, even though the club's domestic matches in the Betway Premiership continue to be officiated without it. South African football fans, in effect, watch their clubs compete with video technology in Africa while going without it every single week at home.

What Fans and Clubs Are Owed Next

For a league that generates significant broadcast revenue and features some of the continent's best-supported clubs, the repeated delay to VAR implementation remains a real frustration for supporters who have been told, more than once, that change was imminent. SAFA's own referees chief has now committed to holding a media briefing once National Executive Committee approval is secured, which should at minimum give fans and clubs a firmer sense of an actual timeline rather than another open-ended promise. Until that happens, the Betway Premiership enters 2026/27 exactly as it has entered every season before it — trusting the same fallible, unassisted eye of the man or woman with the whistle.

How Other Leagues Got There

South Africa is far from the first football nation to discover that VAR is easier to fund than to launch. Leagues across Europe spent a year or more running parallel "shadow" VAR operations — reviewing matches from a studio without any power to intervene — before going live, precisely so that referees, broadcasters and match officials could iron out communication protocols without any actual decisions being affected. It is not yet clear whether SAFA's project team intends to follow a similar phased introduction once training does begin, or whether the plan remains a single, direct switch-on once the National Executive Committee gives its approval. Either approach requires the one thing that has been missing throughout this saga: a hard, published date that fans and clubs can actually hold the federation to.
