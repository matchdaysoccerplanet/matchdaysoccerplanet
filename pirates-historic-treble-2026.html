Orlando Pirates' Historic Treble: How Ouaddou, Appollis and Mofokeng Led the Charge

A first-season treble to match Ruud Krol's 2011 feat — built on a record-breaking defence and a fearless young core.

📅 1 June 2026 ✍️ Matchday Soccer Planet Editorial 🏷️ PSL, Orlando Pirates ⏱️ 4 min read

The Buccaneers' 2025/26 campaign will be remembered as one of the greatest in club history. Moroccan head coach Abdeslam Ouaddou delivered the MTN8, the Carling Knockout Cup, and the Betway Premiership title — a clean sweep of every available domestic trophy in his very first season in charge, matching a feat last managed by club legend Ruud Krol back in 2011.

A Record-Breaking Defence

Pirates finished with a club-record 21 wins from 30 league matches, losing just three times all season and conceding only 12 goals — comfortably the best defensive record in the division. That defensive foundation gave Ouaddou's attacking talents the platform to express themselves without the pressure of chasing games, a luxury few title-winning South African sides have enjoyed in recent memory.

Appollis and Mofokeng Set the League Alight

Going forward, Oswin Appollis and Relebohile Mofokeng were the two most influential attacking players in the Betway Premiership all season. Mofokeng in particular capped his campaign in style — finishing among the league's top scorers before completing a blockbuster move to Belgian side Royale Union Saint-Gilloise shortly after the season ended. Appollis, meanwhile, continued to develop into one of the most exciting wide players outside Europe, combining searching end product with genuine end-of-season consistency.

"A treble in a first season is not something many coaches ever experience. This group deserves every bit of credit for how they handled the pressure of expectation at this club." — Club statement following the Betway Premiership title confirmation

Three Trophies, One Blueprint

The MTN8 and Carling Knockout Cup wins earlier in the season set the tone for the campaign, giving Ouaddou's squad early silverware and belief heading into the longer league grind. Rather than treating the cups as distractions from the league title race, Pirates used them as momentum — arriving at the business end of the Betway Premiership season with a squad already accustomed to winning knockout football under pressure.

What's Next for the Buccaneers

With Mofokeng's departure to Europe, Pirates will need to find fresh attacking creativity for their 2026/27 title defence and their own continental campaign as Betway Premiership champions. The treble has raised expectations at the club to their highest level in over a decade — and Ouaddou will now be judged on whether he can build a squad capable of sustaining this level of success without one of his most influential players.

Rebuilding Around the Treble

Ouaddou and the club's technical team have already moved quickly to reshape the squad for the new campaign, confirming eight new signings alongside seven departures, including surprise exits for previously undroppable names. Recruits have arrived across midfield, defence and the wings, reflecting a deliberate strategy of investing in emerging South African talent while maintaining strong internal competition for places — a philosophy that mirrors how Ouaddou built last season's title-winning squad in the first place.

The Academy Pipeline Continues

Pirates have also continued to lean on their DStv Diski Challenge production line, with six graduates from the club's record-breaking, 75-point DDC title-winning side — including defenders Cristian Derbyshire and Mpho Mphekgoane, left-back option Tshepo Mhlambi, and midfielders Simphiwe Masilela, Lebohang Mohlale and Lungelo Dondolo — training with the first team as pre-season begins. It is a pathway that has already produced Mbekezeli Mbokazi, Relebohile Mofokeng and Mohau Nkota in recent seasons, and gives Ouaddou fresh options as he manages a squad now competing on three fronts.

Ouaddou's Tactical Identity

Much of the treble's foundation was built on defensive solidity rather than pure attacking flair. Pirates' club-record defensive campaign — conceding just 12 goals in 30 league matches — reflected a coach willing to prioritise structure and discipline over expansive, high-risk football, even with attacking talents like Mofokeng, Appollis and Moremi available to him. That balance between defensive organisation and moments of individual brilliance from his most gifted players became the defining feature of Ouaddou's debut season, and explains why replicating it while integrating eight new faces represents such a significant challenge for the campaign ahead.

A Season That Changes Expectations Permanently

Perhaps the most lasting impact of the treble is psychological. Pirates have not merely won trophies; they have reset what success now looks like at Mayfair. Where a strong cup run or a narrow title challenge might once have been considered a good season, Ouaddou's achievement means anything short of genuine trophy contention across multiple competitions will now be viewed as a step backwards by supporters and club hierarchy alike. That shift in expectation, as much as any single signing or departure, may prove to be the treble's most significant and lasting consequence.
