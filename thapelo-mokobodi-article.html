Orlando Pirates Confirm Signing of Thapelo Mokobodi

Buccaneers land Stellenbosch FC midfielder on a three-year deal as their busy transfer window continues.

Matchday Soccer Planet

Orlando Pirates have added more depth to their midfield with the signing of Thapelo Mokobodi from Stellenbosch FC. The 23-year-old has put pen to paper on a three-year contract and will wear the number 33 jersey for the Buccaneers this season, becoming the club's ninth new arrival of the transfer window as the Soweto giants continue building on last season's domestic treble.

Background

Mokobodi's rise has been quick and steady. He began his senior career at Marumo Gallants, then a second-tier side, during the 2023/24 campaign, before earning a move to Stellenbosch FC in August 2025. In just one season in the Cape Winelands, he made 28 appearances for Stellies, including 13 league starts and six outings in the CAF Confederation Cup, catching the eye as one of the country's most promising young defensive midfielders. In total, he has racked up 73 senior appearances in his young career so far.

Ambition

Speaking after putting pen to paper, Mokobodi didn't hide his excitement about the move to Mayfair. "I am pleased to be here and look forward to meeting the rest of the team," he said. "It's a big opportunity to play for such a club, and I am ready to get going. I would like to make a meaningful contribution to this great institution." For a player who was turning out for a second-tier club barely three years ago, pulling on the black and white now represents the biggest step yet in a career he clearly intends to keep building at the highest level of South African football.

Stats at a Glance

What He Adds to the Pirates Setup

Mokobodi joins a Pirates midfield that already has plenty of competition, with the likes of Thalente Mbatha, Makhehlene Makhaula, Masindi Nemtajela, Abdoulaye Mariko and Sihle Nduli all vying for starting spots under coach Abdeslam Ouaddou. What he brings is legs, energy, and experience of high-pressure European-style continental football gained in the Confederation Cup with Stellenbosch. As a defensive midfielder, his job will be to shield the back line, win the ball back quickly, and give the more creative players in front of him a platform to work from. With Nduli also returning from injury, Mokobodi will need to hit the ground running to force his way into the starting XI, but his versatility and work rate give Ouaddou another reliable option in the engine room for a season where Pirates will be fighting on multiple fronts.

Something fans might not know: Despite being known as a defensive, ball-winning midfielder, both of Mokobodi's career goals so far have come against the very same opponent — Black Leopards — scored in separate matches while he was still at Marumo Gallants in the Motsepe Foundation Championship. Pirates fans hoping for the occasional forward burst from their new number 33 at least know he has a taste for finding the net against Leopards.

With the new Betway Premiership season fast approaching, all eyes will be on how quickly Mokobodi can settle into life at one of the continent's biggest clubs — and whether the composure he showed in the Cape Winelands can translate into regular minutes in Soweto.
