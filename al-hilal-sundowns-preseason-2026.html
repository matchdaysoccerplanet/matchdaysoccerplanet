Al Hilal 2-0 Mamelodi Sundowns: What We Learned From a Tough Preseason Test

A much-changed Sundowns side were beaten by a superstar-laden Al Hilal in Austria — but there was plenty for Miguel Cardoso to take from the performance.

📅 26 July 2026 ✍️ Kgomotso Nigel Peu, Independent Sports Journalist 🏷️ Mamelodi Sundowns, Preseason, CAF Champions League ⏱️ 5 min read

Mamelodi Sundowns suffered a 2-0 defeat to Saudi Pro League giants Al Hilal on Friday, 24 July, in a pre-season friendly at the Thermenstadion Stegersbach in Austria. Both goals came from Brazilian winger Malcom, who opened the scoring inside the first ten minutes and doubled the lead early in the second half. On paper it was a comfortable win for the Saudi side. Look closer, though, and there is plenty of context that puts the result in perspective — and a few genuine positives for Sundowns head coach Miguel Cardoso to build on.

A Hugely Depleted Sundowns XI

Context matters here. Sundowns travelled to Austria for a preseason tour and named a significantly weakened squad against Al Hilal, with the club confirming ahead of kick-off that Keegan Johannes, Iqraam Rayners, Thato Sibiya, Marcelo Allende, Bathusi Aubaas, Brayan León, Keanu Cupido and Siyanda Ndlovu were all unavailable for the match. That is the bulk of Cardoso's first-choice attacking and midfield options missing from a single squad list — entirely normal for a preseason tour, where minutes are shared around and fitness is built gradually, but a crucial detail when judging the scoreline.

Al Hilal, by contrast, fielded a strong line-up featuring Karim Benzema, Malcom and Sergej Milinković-Savić, with head coach Simone Inzaghi using the fixture to assess his squad ahead of the new Saudi Pro League season. Darwin Núñez, fresh off representing Uruguay at the 2026 FIFA World Cup, has also recently rejoined the Saudi giants. According to Transfermarkt figures reported by Goal.com, Al Hilal's squad is valued at roughly €176.1 million (around R3.3 billion) against Sundowns' own well-stocked but far less expensive €39.75 million (around R780 million) squad — a gulf of more than four times in market value alone.

The Positives Despite the Scoreline

Given that financial and personnel gap, restricting one of world football's most star-studded attacking lines to two goals — both from the same individual — is a defensible outcome for a much-changed Sundowns side. Malcom's opener came inside the opening ten minutes, but the visitors did not concede again until well into the second half, suggesting a defensive discipline that held up for long spells against constant pressure.

There were also encouraging signs from the days before the Al Hilal match. Two days earlier, Sundowns had opened their Austrian tour with a 1-1 draw against German second-tier side Hertha Berlin, a game in which new signing Antonio van Wyk got off the mark for his new club, and forward Cassius Mailula made his return to action. Both are useful markers for Cardoso as he assesses squad depth heading into a season in which Sundowns will defend their CAF Champions League crown while also chasing back the Betway Premiership title they surrendered to Orlando Pirates after eight straight years on top.

A Squad Still Finding Its Feet After a Difficult Month

It is also worth remembering the emotional backdrop to this pre-season tour. Sundowns are continuing their preparations barely two weeks after the sudden passing of midfielder Jayden Adams on 11 July, a loss that shook the entire club. Simply fielding a full touring squad, blooding new faces such as Van Wyk, and welcoming Mailula back onto the pitch during this period says something about the resilience within the camp, even as the group continues to process its grief.

What's Next

Sundowns will continue their European preparations before returning to South Africa to finalise their squad for the 2026/27 season, in which they will look to reclaim the Betway Premiership title and defend their African crown. A friendly scoreline against one of the best-resourced clubs in world football, while missing the majority of their first-choice XI, is not a result that should cause any alarm in Chloorkop.

Reading Too Much Into Preseason Scorelines

It is worth stressing how limited preseason friendlies are as a predictor of anything meaningful. Fitness levels, tactical experimentation and squad rotation all distort the picture, and clubs at this stage of the calendar are typically far more interested in minutes on the pitch and injury-free returns than in results. Cardoso himself is understood to be using this tour primarily to assess fitness levels across a squad that has had a shortened break following their CAF Champions League triumph in May, rather than to test specific tactical systems against elite opposition. Reading a 2-0 scoreline as a genuine measure of the gap between Sundowns and Al Hilal would be a mistake few serious analysts would make.

What Comes Next on Tour

Sundowns are expected to play at least one further friendly before wrapping up their European preparations and returning to South Africa to finalise their squad ahead of the new campaign. With the Betway Premiership season and a CAF Champions League title defence both on the horizon, every additional minute of match fitness gained on this tour will matter far more in August and September than any single scoreline recorded in July.
