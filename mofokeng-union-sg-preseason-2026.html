Mofokeng's Union Saint-Gilloise Preseason: Debut Assist and What's Next

A first assist off the bench against PSV Eindhoven, a hero's welcome from South African fans, and a Belgian Super Cup date with Club Brugge on the horizon.

📅 26 July 2026 ✍️ Kgomotso Nigel Peu, Independent Sports Journalist 🏷️ Relebohile Mofokeng, Players Abroad, Union Saint-Gilloise ⏱️ 5 min read

Relebohile Mofokeng has begun life at Royale Union Saint-Gilloise the way South African fans hoped he would — making an instant impact the moment he stepped onto the pitch. Since completing his reported R70 million move from Orlando Pirates earlier this month, the Bafana Bafana winger has settled into pre-season training in Belgium and the Netherlands, and already has his first contribution in Union SG colours on the board.

Settling In After a Whirlwind Month

Mofokeng's arrival in Belgium came at the end of a remarkable few weeks. After Bafana Bafana's Round of 32 exit to Canada at the 2026 FIFA World Cup on 28 June, he flew directly from Los Angeles to Brussels rather than returning home with his international teammates, completing his medical and signing a four-year deal with Union Saint-Gilloise. He continued to wear the number 38 shirt he made famous at Orlando Pirates.

After a short break — during which he returned home briefly and hosted a community football tournament through his own foundation in Sharpeville — Mofokeng linked up with his new teammates at Union SG's pre-season training camp in the Netherlands. Footage shared by the club showed him taking part in team-building exercises, including problem-solving challenges designed to help the squad bond, with South African supporters flooding the club's social media with comments celebrating his every appearance.

The Debut: An Assist Off the Bench Against PSV

Mofokeng's first game action for Union SG came on Saturday, 18 July, in a pre-season friendly against Dutch side PSV Eindhoven. He was introduced from the bench for the final 30 minutes with his new side trailing 3-1, and made an immediate impact, setting up a goal as Union SG fought back to secure a 3-3 draw. It was a small sample of minutes, but exactly the kind of impact substitution South African fans have come to expect from him — and his parents shared footage on social media of Union SG supporters chanting his name outside the stadium after the match.

Union SG have played sparingly since Mofokeng's arrival: an earlier pre-season friendly, a 4-0 win over Romanian side FCSB, took place before he had joined full training and did not feature him.

The Bigger Picture: A Champions League Campaign Awaits

Mofokeng's move carries significance beyond his own career. Union Saint-Gilloise finished as runners-up in the 2025/26 Belgian Pro League, behind champions Club Brugge, and have qualified for the third qualifying round of the 2026/27 UEFA Champions League, where they will face Norwegian side Bodø/Glimt — last season's surprise Round of 16 team, who eliminated both Manchester City and Atletico Madrid during the competition's league phase. Should Union SG progress and Mofokeng feature, he would follow South Africans Benni McCarthy, Nasief Morris, Lucas Radebe and Tsepo Masilela as Bafana Bafana representatives to have played in the tournament.

What's Next

Before any European qualifiers, Mofokeng and his new teammates face an immediate test: Union Saint-Gilloise's first official fixture of the season comes in the Belgian Super Cup against champions Club Brugge on Friday, 31 July. It is likely to be Mofokeng's first taste of a competitive match in Belgian colours, and Matchday Soccer Planet will be following his progress closely as his European adventure begins in earnest.

Adjusting to a New Football Culture

Beyond the pitch, Mofokeng's early weeks in Belgium have also been about cultural adjustment. Union Saint-Gilloise's pre-season programme in the Netherlands placed heavy emphasis on squad bonding exercises alongside the football itself, reflecting a European club culture that often prioritises integrating new signings socially as much as tactically. For a 21-year-old moving continents for the first time, immediately after the emotional high of a World Cup campaign, that early focus on team chemistry may prove just as valuable as his minutes on the pitch in determining how quickly he settles into Belgian football.

The Weight of Expectation Back Home

South African football fans have followed Mofokeng's every move since his transfer was confirmed with an intensity rarely seen for a player moving to a club outside Europe's traditional "big five" leagues. That reflects both his growing standing as one of the country's most exciting attacking talents and the broader hunger among South African supporters to see homegrown players succeed on a genuinely competitive European stage. How he performs in the coming weeks, particularly if he features in the Champions League qualifiers, will likely shape the next chapter of that narrative significantly.
