Mamelodi Sundowns' CAF Champions League Triumph: A Decade of Determination

After years of continental heartbreak, the Brazilians conquered Africa again — beating AS FAR Rabat 2-1 on aggregate.

📅 30 May 2026 ✍️ Matchday Soccer Planet Editorial 🏷️ CAF, Mamelodi Sundowns ⏱️ 4 min read

The journey to continental glory has been long and painful for the Brazilians. After lifting the CAF Champions League trophy in 2016 under Pitso Mosimane, Mamelodi Sundowns dominated domestically for almost a decade but kept falling at the final continental hurdle — losing finals, semi-finals, and heartbreaking away-goal deciders along the way. On 30 May 2026, a decade of near-misses was finally rewarded.

Cardoso's Tactical Masterclass

Portuguese head coach Miguel Cardoso guided Sundowns to a 1-0 first-leg win at Loftus Versfeld before a disciplined 1-1 draw in Rabat sealed a 2-1 aggregate victory over AS FAR. Cardoso, appointed midway through the previous season, built a Sundowns side capable of controlling matches away from home — a trait that had eluded the club in previous continental campaigns where they were often eliminated on the road.

The away draw in Morocco was a study in game management: Sundowns absorbed pressure for long spells, protected their first-leg advantage, and struck on the counter when the opportunity arose. It was a mature, patient performance from a squad that had previously been accused of losing composure in the biggest continental moments.

A Season of Contradictions

The triumph completes a remarkable and slightly unusual season for Sundowns — a club that finished runners-up domestically in the Betway Premiership, just one point behind Orlando Pirates, but ended the campaign as kings of Africa. It's a reminder that continental and domestic form don't always move in lockstep, and that squad depth built for two competitions can sometimes come at the cost of a bit of consistency in the league.

Key contributors throughout the continental run included the same core of players who featured prominently in the Betway Premiership table — with Iqraam Rayners' goalscoring form and a settled defensive unit anchored by experienced campaigners proving decisive in the tightest continental fixtures.

What This Means for South African Football

Sundowns' second CAF Champions League crown cements their status as the dominant South African club on the continental stage over the past decade, and qualifies them for the 2026 FIFA Club World Cup qualification pathway. For a Betway Premiership that has produced a genuinely competitive title race and now a continental champion in the same season, it's further evidence that South African club football's standing in Africa remains strong even as individual stars increasingly move to Europe.

Defending the Crown in 2026/27

Attention has already turned to how Cardoso rebuilds his squad for a title defence on two fronts. Sundowns have moved for Austrian-based winger Antonio van Wyk from SV Ried and versatile attacker Siyanda Ndlovu from Golden Arrows, with both already involved in pre-season training in Europe ahead of the new campaign. The club has also continued to lean on its academy, with 19-year-old striker Bennet Mokoena — the DStv Diski Challenge's top scorer with 22 goals the previous season — promoted into first-team contention after making his senior debut and contributing a goal in a friendly win over Bundesliga side RB Leipzig.

The squad's pre-season has not been without difficulty. Sundowns are continuing their preparations only weeks after the sudden passing of midfielder Jayden Adams in July, a loss felt deeply across the club. Balancing that grief with the demands of defending both a domestic title challenge and a continental crown will be one of the more difficult tasks Cardoso has faced since taking charge.

Cardoso's Tactical Fingerprint

Much of the credit for ending Sundowns' near-decade continental wait belongs to head coach Miguel Cardoso, whose methodical, possession-based approach marked a departure from some of his predecessors' more direct styles. The Portuguese coach placed heavy emphasis on patient build-up play and defensive organisation across two legs against AS FAR, a template that proved decisive in a tie ultimately settled by a narrow aggregate margin. That tactical discipline under pressure, in what was effectively a cup final spread across two matches, is likely to remain the template Cardoso leans on as Sundowns look to balance continental and domestic commitments in the season ahead.

The Financial and Reputational Dividend

Beyond the trophy itself, the CAF Champions League title carries significant financial and reputational value for Sundowns. Continental prize money, increased broadcast exposure across Africa, and a stronger negotiating position for future player sales all flow from establishing the club as Africa's premier side once again. It also strengthens Sundowns' case as the continent's most consistent big-game performer over the past decade, a reputation that helps attract the calibre of coaching staff and playing talent — including European-based recruits like Antonio van Wyk — willing to join a club with a genuine claim to being among Africa's elite.
